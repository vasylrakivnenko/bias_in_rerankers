# Target journals — "Gender Bias in Automated Hiring — the Case for De-Identification"

Paper length: ~6,100 words excl. bibliography / ~7,000 words incl. bibliography
(computed from `main.tex`, 2026-09-04). All portals below use Scholastica unless
noted, so a single submission list can go out simultaneously (standard law
review practice) — see notes on expedited review.

## Strong fit, open now

- **Colorado Technology Law Journal** — rolling submissions, no fixed window,
  tech-law focused. Already a cited venue in the paper (Gerards & Zuiderveen
  Borgesius, `@gerards2022`). Submit via Scholastica or ctlj@colorado.edu.
- **Texas Law Review Online (TLRO)** — open year-round. Wants 3,000–11,000
  words incl. footnotes. Comfortable fit.
- **Columbia Law Review Forum (CLR Forum)** — rolling via Scholastica.
  Prefers 3,000–12,000 words incl. footnotes. Comfortable fit.
- **University of Chicago Law Review Online** — submissions open per their
  site. Via Scholastica or lrarticles@law.uchicago.edu.
- **Michigan Law Review Online** — currently open. Explicitly wants pieces
  "accessible to a general audience of practitioners and policymakers" —
  matches this paper's house style well.

## Possible, narrower fit

- **Yale Law Journal Forum** — open on a rolling basis (portal reopened
  28 Jan 2026). Sweet spot is 6,000–10,000 words, hard max 14,000 — our
  ~7,000 words actually lands right in range now that the real count is in.
- **Berkeley Technology Law Journal — Commentaries** — open for the Fall
  2026 cycle. Main BTLJ track doesn't take short pieces at all; Commentaries
  caps around ~3,000 words, so this draft would need real cutting, not just
  reformatting.
- **Duke Law Journal Online** — accepts unsolicited short pieces, but their
  stated range is 2,000–5,000 words; our current length runs over, and
  solicited pieces reportedly get priority.

## Lower-ranked general-interest, open now (backup options)

- **Lincoln Memorial University Law Review** — general-interest student-run
  review (Vol. 14, Issue 1 currently accepting submissions per author, checked
  9/5/2026). No hard word-count wall found in their public guidelines; broad
  subject scope (constitutional law, criminal justice, etc.), not tech-law
  specialized. Lower prestige/ranking than the "strong fit" venues above — use
  as a fallback if the higher-ranked venues all pass, not a first choice.
  Submit via Scholastica (lmulawreview.scholasticahq.com).

## Closed / not pursuing

- **Harvard Law & Policy Review** — ruled out on format: the print Articles
  track wants ~15,000–30,000 words (more than double our length) with a
  domestic-policy-innovation framing; "HLPR Online" wants 1,000–2,000-word
  informal magazine essays with hyperlinks in place of footnotes. Neither
  track fits a ~6,100-word footnoted academic piece without a substantial
  rewrite. (Topical fit — U.S. domestic policy — is otherwise reasonable;
  revisit only if willing to cut to a ~1,500-word magazine-style version for
  Online.)
- **NYU Law Review Online** — closed, reopens Fall 2026.
- **Harvard Law Review Forum** — Essays track wants 12,000–17,500 words
  (too long to fit without padding); the shorter Commentaries track
  (6,000 words) is "typically solicited," not open to unsolicited pieces.
- **Stanford Technology Law Review (main Articles track)** — ruled out:
  Articles run 15,000–25,000 words (cap 30,000), built for full-length
  doctrinal pieces, not this short empirical format. Notes track is
  restricted to current law students, so not available to a faculty author.
  (Strong topical fit otherwise — STLR published Houser 2019, the paper this
  one extends — but the Articles-track length mismatch rules it out for now.)
- **Stanford Law Review Online** — closed (per author, checked directly).

## Process notes

- Submit to all "strong fit" + any desired "possible fit" venues
  simultaneously via Scholastica — this is standard/expected practice for
  law review submissions, unlike most academic fields.
- Once any journal makes an offer, email the higher-ranked journals still
  pending to request expedited review by a stated deadline (typically
  24–72 hours). Only withdraw from other journals after formally accepting
  one offer.
- Cover note draft: `legal_paper/cover_note.txt` (needs title/affiliation
  and email filled in before sending).
